 Telleca Telecom | Insights

Analysis period: January 2022 – September 2025

 1. Customer & Revenue Growth

Telleca's customer and revenue measures show substantial growth across the period analysed.

Revenue increased from approximately ZMW 166 thousand in 2022 to ZMW 2.26 million in 2023, ZMW 10.11 million in 2024, and ZMW 21.95 million in 2025 year-to-date.

Total revenue across the available period is approximately ZMW 34.49 million.

Revenue is geographically concentrated. Lusaka contributes approximately ZMW 10.35 million, while Copperbelt contributes approximately ZMW 7.82 million. Together, the two provinces account for approximately 52.7% of total revenue.

The distribution provides a useful starting point for understanding where Telleca's commercial activity is concentrated.

---

 2. Customer Profile

The customer base is predominantly made up of individual customers.

| Customer Type |  Share |
| ------------- | -----: |
| Individual    | 87.88% |
| Business      | 10.15% |
| Government    |  1.97% |

Retail stores and online channels account for a significant proportion of customer acquisition.

The gender distribution is relatively balanced, with approximately 10,325 male and 9,818 female customers.

The relatively small difference in churn rates between male and female customers suggests that gender alone does not explain a substantial difference in churn within this dataset.

 3. Plan & Usage Behaviour

Customer usage varies considerably across Telleca's plans.

| Plan         | Avg. Data (GB) | Avg. Voice (mins) | Avg. SMS |
| ------------ | -------------: | ----------------: | -------: |
| Enterprise   |         298.94 |             8,296 |   17,668 |
| Home Max     |         245.30 |                 0 |        0 |
| Business Pro |         143.55 |             3,958 |   10,006 |
| Lite         |           5.68 |               137 |      271 |
| Start        |           2.21 |                49 |       48 |

Enterprise customers record the highest average usage across all three measures.

The difference between enterprise/business plans and lower-tier consumer plans is substantial, particularly for data and voice usage.

This demonstrates the importance of considering customer value and usage alongside subscriber volume when analysing the plan portfolio.

 4. Churn

There are 4,435 recorded churned customers, equivalent to an overall churn rate of approximately 22.02% of the customer base.

Churn varies considerably by contract type.

| Contract Type | Churn Rate |
| ------------- | ---------: |
| Prepaid       |     39.08% |
| Postpaid      |     17.96% |
| 12-month      |      9.48% |
| 24-month      |      6.95% |

The data shows a clear difference between prepaid customers and customers on longer contractual arrangements.

This is an observed association in the dataset; it does not by itself establish that contract length causes the difference in churn.

---

 5. Churn Reasons

The most frequently recorded churn reasons are:

| Churn Reason            |  Share |
| ----------------------- | -----: |
| Better competitor offer | 26.97% |
| Price too high          | 26.85% |
| Poor coverage           | 12.97% |
| Service quality         | 11.97% |
| Non-payment             |  6.61% |

The first two categories account for approximately 53.8% of recorded churn reasons.

This indicates that pricing and competitive offers are prominent features of the churn data.

At the same time, network coverage and service quality together account for a meaningful proportion of churn reasons, meaning the churn picture is not exclusively commercial.

---

 6. Churn by Plan

Churn rates differ across individual plans.

The highest observed rates include:

| Plan          | Churn Rate |
| ------------- | ---------: |
| Telleca Start |     60.00% |
| Telleca Lite  |     44.16% |
| Telleca Youth |     41.64% |

These percentages should be interpreted together with subscriber volumes.

A high churn rate on a small customer group does not necessarily represent the same business impact as a lower churn rate across a much larger customer population.

---

 7. Churn by Province

Lusaka and Copperbelt account for a large proportion of both overall revenue and churned customers.

The recorded churn rates are approximately:

 Lusaka — 28.91%
 Copperbelt — 23.79%

The geographic pattern makes these provinces important areas for further investigation.

Further analysis could examine whether the higher churn reflects differences in customer mix, plan selection, competition, pricing, network experience or other factors.

---

 8. Retention Activity

The dataset contains 2,283 retention attempts.

Of these:

 830 were accepted
 1,453 were not accepted
 Acceptance rate: 36.4%

This provides a useful measure of retention activity, but acceptance should not be treated as equivalent to successful long-term retention.

A stronger retention analysis would track customers after an accepted offer and measure whether they remained active over a defined period.

---

 9. Service & Network Performance

Telleca recorded 6,118 network incidents during the analysis period.

The average recorded resolution time was approximately 17.79 hours.

Customer service interactions were distributed as follows:

| Resolution Status |  Share |
| ----------------- | -----: |
| Resolved          | 72.24% |
| Escalated         | 16.65% |
| Unresolved        | 11.11% |

Service quality appears in the churn data as one of the recorded reasons customers leave.

The relationship between service interactions, network incidents and churn would require customer-level event data and appropriate time windows to establish whether individual service experiences preceded churn.

---

 10. Overall Observations

Several themes emerge from the analysis:

 Growth

The customer and revenue base expanded considerably during the period covered by the dataset.

 Customer Mix

Telleca's customer base is predominantly individual customers, with business and government customers representing smaller segments.

 Usage

Usage differs substantially between plans, with enterprise and business customers recording considerably higher average consumption.

 Churn

Churn represents a significant portion of the customer population and varies considerably across contract types and plans.

 Churn Drivers

Pricing and competitor offers are the two most frequently recorded churn reasons, while coverage and service quality also feature prominently.

 Retention

Retention attempts can be measured, but additional outcome data would be required to determine whether accepted retention offers translate into sustained customer retention.

 Geography

Revenue and churn are concentrated in a relatively small number of provinces, providing an opportunity for more detailed regional analysis.

---

 11. Areas for Further Analysis

The current report establishes the main patterns in the dataset. Several areas could be developed further:

 Customer lifetime value
 Revenue per customer
 Customer tenure and churn
 Churn by customer value
 Churn by acquisition channel
 Monthly cohort retention
 Retention offer performance over time
 Relationship between network incidents and individual churn
 Plan migration between subscription types
 Customer-level churn prediction

These would extend the project from descriptive reporting into more advanced customer analytics.
